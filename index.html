— 01 — what this actually is

it's a media gig, not a flyer gig.

most campus ambassador programs hand you a box of stickers and a graphic to repost. this one is closer to running a small publication. you get a brand, a content playbook, a budget, and a cut of everyone you bring in. here's the honest difference.

the usual ambassador gig

  • Hand out branded water bottles at the quad
  • Repost a graphic the marketing team wrote
  • Flat fee, or just free merch
  • No real skill built, nothing to show afterward
  • Program ends, you get a line on your résumé
  • You're a distribution channel for someone else

fomo campus

  • You run an account in your own voice, about your own school
  • We give hooks and templates — the calls are yours
  • Cash bounties for completing real tasks
  • You learn content, distribution and analytics for real
  • The audience you build is yours, and it follows you out
  • You're building something. fomo sponsors it.

— 02 — what it pays

one way you get paid: cash bounties for completing specific tasks on the ladder below. each bounty has a fixed payout and a clear proof requirement. no points, no credits, no gift cards — actual money.

— 06 — the playbook

how the good ones do it.

reverse-engineered from the ambassadors currently doing the best numbers. none of it is theory, and none of it requires you to be good on camera on day one.

your first week

A realistic seven days. Ship all of it and you'll be ahead of most people who start the same month as you.

day 1

Claim your handle on three platforms. Same name everywhere. Write a six-word bio.

day 2

Film three "what's in your portfolio" streets in one 40-minute session. Bank them.

day 3

Post #1. Open the group chat. Personally invite 30 people — by DM, not by story.

day 4

Post #2. Reply to every comment within an hour. That matters more than the edit.

day 5

Put your referral link in your bio and pin it in the chat. First signups land here.

day 6

Post the tuition chart. Highest-share asset in the kit — use it to spike your reach.

day 7

Check your analytics in the hub. Kill your worst format, double down on your best.

"Finance content" isn't a channel. "The money desk for Ohio State" is. Your beat is where markets meet one specific campus, and ideally nothing you post could have been posted by someone at another school.

  • Name it like a publication, not a username
  • Pick one recurring segment you can run 20 times
  • If a national account could post it, rewrite it

Nobody decides to keep watching at second ten. Open on the number, the reaction, or the question — never on your name or a logo. If the first three seconds could be cut with no loss, cut them.

Beat reporters show up whether or not something big happened. Consistency beats virality over a semester — five ordinary posts a week outperforms one great post a month, every time.

Views don't pay bounties — verified signups do. Pin your referral link, say it out loud in every video, and give people a specific reason to tap it today, not "eventually."

The fastest-growing channels aren't solo. Bring on a teammate to split filming and editing, and you'll both post more often without either of you burning out.

— 11 — is this for you

the honest version.

this works really well for some people and not at all for others. we'd rather you work out which one you are now than three weeks in.

a good fit if

  • You're already the person your friends ask about money, stocks or crypto
  • You can give it 4–6 hours a week consistently, including exam weeks
  • You're comfortable posting with your face and name attached
  • You want to learn content and distribution, not just make beer money
  • You're socially connected on campus — clubs, greek life, sports, dorm life
  • You'd rather be paid on results than on hours

a bad fit if

  • You want guaranteed income regardless of output — this is performance-based
  • You're hoping to post once a month and still get paid
  • You'd rather not be on camera or in a group chat daily
  • You'd feel weird recommending a trading app to your friends
  • You want a passive résumé line rather than actual work
  • Your schedule is already fully committed this semester
